# Sample 3: Reward / Curiosity Bait (Fictional Example)

> This is a fictional, generic mock-up for educational analysis only. It does
> not impersonate any real company, contest, or promotion.

## The Email

```
From: Rewards Center <rewards@prize-notify-center.com>
To: user@example.com
Subject: Congratulations! You've won a $500 gift card 🎉

Hello Winner,

You've been randomly selected to receive a $500 gift card as part of our
loyalty rewards program! This offer expires in 2 hours, so claim it now
before it's gone.

[Claim Your Prize]  (placeholder — not a real link)

You'll just need to fill out a short form with your name, email, and
billing details to confirm delivery.

Congratulations again!
Rewards Center Team
```

## Red Flags Identified

| # | Red Flag | Explanation |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Sender domain `prize-notify-center.com` | Generic, unaffiliated with any real brand |
| 2 | "Randomly selected" for a reward you never entered | You can't win a contest you never entered |
| 3 | Short expiry window ("2 hours") | Manufactured urgency to prevent research/verification |
| 4 | Request for "billing details" to claim a "free" prize | Legitimate free rewards never require payment/billing info |
| 5 | Generic "Hello Winner" greeting | No personalization |
| 6 | Emoji + exclamation-heavy tone | Designed to trigger excitement over caution |

## Social Engineering Technique
**Reward + Curiosity + Urgency** — dangling something desirable (money,
prizes) to lower the recipient's guard, then requesting sensitive
information under the guise of "claiming" it.

## Correct Response
Delete/report. Legitimate rewards or loyalty programs don't ask for billing
information via unsolicited email, and don't require action within an
artificially short window.
